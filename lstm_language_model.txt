Approach

I built a word-level LSTM language model from scratch and connected it to a small Gradio page. The model estimates the probability of the next word given the words so far, and I train it by increasing the probability of the real next word. Perplexity is exp of the average cross-entropy, so a lower score means the model is less surprised by the real text.

The network is an embedding of size 200, a 2-layer LSTM with hidden size 200 and dropout 0.2, and a linear layer over the vocabulary. I train for 8 epochs with AdamW at learning rate 0.001 and clip the gradients at 1.0. I cut the corpus into 20 streams and use windows of 35 tokens. The target is that window shifted forward by one. I keep the hidden state across windows but detach it, so the model remembers earlier tokens while backpropagation stays inside the window. I save the checkpoint only when validation perplexity improves.

For generation I tokenize the prompt, read the distribution at the last position, pick a word, and feed that word back in. Temperature 0 always takes the most likely word. A higher temperature makes less likely words easier to sample. The page lets me enter a starting phrase, choose how many words to generate, set the temperature, and see the 10 most probable next words.

Dataset

I used WikiText-2 raw, the Wikipedia articles in the Salesforce release. The text is already split on spaces, but some punctuation is stored as @-@, @,@, and @.@, so I turn those back into -, ,, and . before splitting. I drop blank lines and article headings, and I put <eos> at the end of each line. I build the vocabulary from the training split only and keep a word if it appears at least 3 times. Everything else becomes <unk>. That gives me 32,993 words.

What I learned

A language model is a product of next-word probabilities, and the LSTM stores the prefix in its hidden state instead of looking at a fixed window the way an n-gram does. My add-1 bigram, which only sees the previous word, has a validation perplexity of about 2228. The LSTM reaches 221.53 on validation and 200.76 on test, down from 371.13 after the first epoch.

I also learned that scoring the real text and generating text are different. Perplexity always uses the real previous words. Generation has to live with its own choices. At temperature 0, "The history of science" collapses into hyphens and <unk>, because the top word is a hyphen and the word after a hyphen is often rare. At 0.8 and 1.2 the continuation sounds locally like Wikipedia, but it does not stay on one topic. The next-word list does change with the prompt: after this one, fiction is near the top.

Other details

The full code is in lstm_language_model.ipynb. I worked through it on my own. Training takes about 20 minutes on my Mac's GPU, and the notebook should be run from the project folder so the data and the checkpoint are saved next to it.
